# Development guide references

Reviewed on 2026-09-07. This app contains a selected educational checklist, not a validated screening or diagnostic instrument. A missing checkmark means a parent has not recorded a skill; it does not establish that the skill is absent.

The following CDC pages support the corrected reference ages and descriptions:

- [6 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/6-months.html): supported sitting, tummy-to-back rolling, vocal turn-taking and reaching.
- [9 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/9-months.html): unsupported sitting, transferring/raking objects, babbling and responding to a name.
- [12 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html): pulling to stand, furniture-supported walking and understanding “no”.
- [15 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/15-months.html): a few independent steps and trying one or two words beyond parent names.
- [18 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/18-months.html): independent walking, trying at least three words beyond parent names, sharing interest by pointing and attempting spoon use.
- [2 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html): two-word combinations, identifying body parts, spoon use and noticing distress in others.
- [30 months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/30-months.html): two-step instructions and recognizing a color.
- [4 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html): catching a large ball, pencil grip, drawing a person, colors and familiar story sequences.
- [5 years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html): storytelling, conversation, hopping and turn-taking.

Additional references:

- [AAP/ASHA: multilingual development](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/7-Myths-Facts-Bilingual-Children-Learning-Language.aspx): multiple languages do not themselves cause language delay; assessment considers all languages.
- [AAP: movement at 8–12 months](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/Movement-8-to-12-Months.aspx): variation in crawling and movement.
- [NHS: urgent help for young children](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/health/when-to-get-urgent-medical-help-for-babies-and-children-under-5/): breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness and seizures require urgent emergency attention. The app directs users to their local emergency service, not a country-specific number.

Chat replies separate reference ages from the child’s current age, suppress future-age warning signs, and direct concerns or loss of acquired skills to a clinician. The local matcher is limited to development topics and is not an exhaustive medical triage system. The existing differential-cause lists in the stage screen are educational possibilities, not diagnoses of an individual child.

Age-aware coaching uses skill progressions: sounds and vocal turn-taking before meaningful words; head control and supported sitting before unsupported sitting; and supported movement before independent steps. For example, speech coaching at 7 months uses the 6-month vocalization reference, not the 12- or 15-month word checklist. “Not yet expected” is an interpretation of the reference ages, not a claim that early words are impossible. Reference ages describe skills most children can do by that age, not exact onset dates or a diagnosis. Sound imitation and picture-book interaction follow the CDC parent tips above. Reports of absent current skills, hearing concerns or lost vocalization take priority over reassurance about later words.

Some checklist entries were substantively corrected. Their IDs have a `-v2` suffix so an old checkmark cannot silently mark a different skill as achieved. Unchanged skills keep their IDs and saved checkmarks.
